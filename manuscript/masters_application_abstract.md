# Research summary for graduate application

I developed a reproducible bioinformatics and machine-learning workflow to identify DNA methylation biomarkers for endometrial cancer detection using public methylation-array datasets. The discovery cohort consisted of TCGA-UCEC Illumina HumanMethylation450 data, including 420 tumor and 46 normal endometrial tissue samples. After quality control, 390,516 probes across 466 samples were retained. I performed differential methylation analysis, restricted candidate markers to promoter-region hypermethylated probes, integrated prior biological evidence, and used LASSO-based feature selection with logistic regression modeling.

The final panel included ten genes: six literature-constrained markers (CDO1, PAX1, BHLHE22, HAND2, TBX5, and ZNF454) and four data-driven markers (CYP26C1, SPARCL1, WDR52, and CLDN15). To reduce optimism from feature-selection leakage, I implemented a fully nested cross-validation framework in which differential methylation filtering, promoter restriction, LASSO feature selection, and model training were repeated inside each training fold. The nested TCGA evaluation achieved an AUC of 0.9989 ± 0.0024, with sensitivity of 0.9976 and specificity of 0.9778. The fixed ten-gene panel was externally validated in GEO GSE155760, containing 33 endometrial cancer and 13 normal endometrial samples profiled on the EPIC methylation array. All ten probes were present in the external dataset, and the TCGA-trained logistic regression model achieved an AUC of 0.9790, sensitivity of 0.9091, and specificity of 1.0000.

This project strengthened my skills in high-dimensional omics analysis, leakage-aware machine learning, external validation, and reproducible scientific software development. It also taught me how to balance data-driven discovery with biological interpretability, a principle I hope to further develop in graduate study.
